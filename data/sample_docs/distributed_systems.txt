Distributed Systems Fundamentals

Distributed systems coordinate multiple independent computers to appear as a single coherent system. Consensus algorithms such as Raft and Paxos ensure agreement on shared state despite failures. Leader election, log replication, and safety properties are central to correctness.

Fault tolerance relies on replication and redundancy. Quorum-based reads and writes balance consistency and availability. Partition tolerance requires careful trade-offs described by the CAP theorem: consistency, availability, and partition tolerance cannot all be maximized simultaneously.

Message passing, idempotency, and exactly-once semantics are critical for reliable communication. Network partitions, clock skew, and partial failures make distributed debugging challenging. Observability via tracing and metrics is essential for production systems.

Scaling strategies include horizontal sharding, consistent hashing, and load balancing. Data locality and cache coherence affect latency. Backpressure and circuit breakers prevent cascading failures under load.
